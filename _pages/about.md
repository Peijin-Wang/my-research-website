I am a PhD candidate in Biostatistics at Duke University, advised by Dr. Hwanhee Hong. My research develops Bayesian and causal inference methods for clinical trials, with an emphasis on incorporating external control data into randomized studies.

My current work focuses on dynamic borrowing, hybrid controlled trials, and the use of real-world data and real-world evidence in drug development. I am particularly interested in methods that improve the efficiency of clinical research while preserving robust and interpretable treatment-effect estimation. My applied collaborations span chronic lung allograft dysfunction, atrial fibrillation, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis.

Before beginning my doctoral studies, I earned an MS in Biostatistics from Duke University and a BS in Statistics from Central South University. I expect to complete my PhD in May 2027.
